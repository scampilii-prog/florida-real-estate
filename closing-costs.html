What Goes Into Closing Costs?

Closing costs depend on the contract, property, financing, county, insurance and closing date. Use the detailed calculators above for itemized estimates; the following checklist helps you understand what to confirm before closing.

For Buyers

  • Loan origination, underwriting, discount points and lender charges, if financing.
  • Appraisal, credit report, inspections and survey when applicable.
  • Title services, lender's title insurance and recording charges, as allocated by contract.
  • Prepaid interest, homeowners and flood insurance, escrow reserves and property-tax adjustments.
  • Condominium or HOA application and transfer charges, if applicable.

For Sellers

  • Existing mortgage balance and payoff fees, including any required lien satisfaction.
  • Florida documentary stamp tax on the deed, subject to exemptions or special circumstances.
  • Title, settlement, recording, estoppel and association fees according to the contract.
  • Property tax, HOA and condominium assessment prorations.
  • Brokerage compensation or concessions only as negotiated and agreed in writing.

Florida Closing Questions Worth Asking

Are buyer and seller closing costs the same?

No. Each party has different expenses. Who pays certain items, including title-related charges, depends on the purchase contract and local arrangements.

Does the purchase price tell me the exact amount?

No. Final numbers depend on the transaction's details and the lender's Closing Disclosure or settlement statement. A worksheet is a planning estimate, not a final quote.

What changes for a cash purchase?

Cash purchases avoid mortgage-related charges but can still involve inspections, title services, transfer or recording fees, insurance and property prorations.

Are there additional coastal property expenses?

Flood and wind insurance, property inspections, seawall or dock due diligence, condominium assessments and association requirements may be important, depending on the home.

When are final figures available?

For most consumer mortgage loans, the lender generally must provide the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before consummation. The settlement agent confirms the final figures and payment instructions.

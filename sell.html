A thoughtful selling strategy

More than putting a home on the market

Every property deserves a plan based on its condition, location, competition, and the seller's priorities. The right approach may differ for a waterfront residence, condo, new home, or investment property.

01 · Price with evidence

Review comparable sales, active competition, property features, and likely buyer expectations. An asking price is a strategy, not a guarantee.

02 · Prepare and present

Identify worthwhile repairs, staging opportunities, photography needs, and accurate property information before launching.

03 · Choose your exposure

Discuss the marketing plan, timing, privacy preferences, and available distribution channels. Specific advertising services depend on the agreed listing plan.

04 · Review offers carefully

Consider financing strength, deposits, contingencies, inspection periods, closing dates, and estimated net proceeds—not price alone.

05 · Navigate due diligence

Coordinate communications about inspections, association requirements, disclosures, appraisal, and contract deadlines with the appropriate professionals.

06 · Stay informed through closing

Agree on a practical communication schedule for showings, feedback, offers, and milestones. Updates are provided through the channels arranged with Sabatino.

Step 06 · From signed contract to closing

What Happens After You Accept an Offer?

Once both parties sign, deadlines become critical. Your actual purchase contract and any addenda—not a generic timeline—control which obligations apply and when they are due. These are the main checkpoints to review with your agent and closing professional.

1 · Effective date & deposit

Confirm the contract's effective date, escrow holder, amount of the buyer's earnest money, and the deposit delivery deadline. Retain confirmation of receipt from the escrow holder.

Review: Fully signed contract, escrow instructions and deposit receipt.

2 · Inspection period

Identify the inspection deadline, access arrangements and how repair requests or cancellation rights work under the selected contract. Do not assume the period uses business days; check the wording.

Review: Inspection provisions, notices and any agreed repair addenda.

3 · Financing & appraisal

For a financed offer, track loan-application and approval deadlines, appraisal progress and any applicable financing contingency. A preapproval or DU/LPA result does not guarantee the buyer's loan will close.

Review: Financing and appraisal clauses, lender updates and required notices.

4 · Title, survey & liens

The closing professional checks ownership, liens and other title matters. Clarify who orders and pays for any survey, and allow time to address contractually required title objections or outstanding obligations.

Review: Title commitment, payoff statements, survey and curative documents as applicable.

5 · Association & property requirements

Confirm HOA or condominium approvals, estoppels, applicable disclosures, assessments and any permit or inspection follow-up. These steps can affect timing and costs.

Review: Association approval status, fees and required supporting records.

6 · Final walkthrough & possession

Confirm the agreed property condition, included items, final walkthrough arrangements, keys and occupancy terms. Any post-closing possession should be documented rather than assumed.

Review: Contract terms, walkthrough arrangements and possession instructions.

7 · Settlement & closing

Review the closing statement, mortgage payoff, agreed commissions, documentary stamp tax, prorations and other seller charges with the closing professional before signing.

Review: Settlement statement, deed, required affidavits and disbursement instructions.

Step 05 · Comparing offers

The Best Offer Is Not Always the Highest Price

When reviewing a purchase offer, look at what the seller may actually receive, the likelihood of closing, and the obligations the contract creates. Two buyers offering different prices can produce surprisingly similar results.

Price & net proceeds

Subtract proposed seller credits, requested repairs, agreed compensation, applicable closing expenses and any other negotiated seller costs.

Deposit & deadlines

Check earnest money amount, when it must be delivered, inspection periods, financing deadlines, and remedies under the actual contract. A deposit is not automatically nonrefundable.

Financing & appraisal

Compare cash versus financed terms, evidence of funds, lender preapproval and financing or appraisal contingencies. DU or LPA findings are conditional underwriting results, not a promise to fund.

Closing & possession

Consider the closing date, association approvals, title and survey issues, included fixtures, occupancy arrangements and any unusually complex conditions.

Step 04 · Marketing & showings

How Will We Market Your Property?

A good marketing plan begins with the right audience and an agreed level of exposure. Here are the decisions worth discussing before your listing goes live.

1 · Public MLS exposure

Discuss an MLS listing, property descriptions, photography, availability for showings and distribution to participating real estate websites. Publication and syndication depend on brokerage and MLS rules.

2 · Targeted presentation

Agree on an appropriate mix of photographs, floor plans, videos, property details and broker outreach. Professional media and advertising are considered individually, not assumed to be included.

3 · More privacy, less exposure

If privacy is a priority, ask about a more limited marketing approach and how that may reduce visibility or buyer competition. Any restricted marketing must follow applicable MLS, brokerage and listing-agreement requirements.

4 · Showings & security

Decide how much notice you need, available days and times, access instructions, pet arrangements and how to safeguard valuables and personal information. Confirm association restrictions before scheduling.

What happens after the listing launches?

  1. Review inquiries: Determine which questions or showing requests need follow-up.
  2. Discuss feedback: Evaluate comments in context; feedback alone does not establish market value.
  3. Monitor comparable listings: Review meaningful changes in inventory, pending sales, and competing asking prices.
  4. Revisit the plan: Discuss pricing, presentation or exposure changes when the market response supports them.

Questions to ask before signing a listing agreement

Which marketing channels will actually be used? Who pays for photography or advertising? How will showings be scheduled? How frequently will we discuss activity and feedback? What restrictions apply if I want a private sale? How will potential offers be presented?
